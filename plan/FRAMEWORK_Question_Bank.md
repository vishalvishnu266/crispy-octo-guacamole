# FRAMEWORK – "Question-First" Weekly Learning System (UPSC Prelims)

> **Reference document.** Every weekly question bank follows this framework.
> Main plan: `D:\last\plan\UPSC_Prelims_Detailed_Plan.md`
> Weekly files: `D:\last\plan\Week_XX\WeekXX_Questions_and_Answers.md`
> **Formatting rule (mobile reading):** no tables. Use bullet lists, ❌/✅ trap pairs, and one bold-titled "card" per item instead.

---

## 1. Core Idea

**Questions come before answers.** Before you read a topic, you face questions on it from many angles. This:
1. **Shows where you really stand** (diagnosis, before any learning)
2. **Primes the brain**, so you read the book looking for answers instead of passively
3. **Trains UPSC thinking:** recent papers test concepts, exceptions, and statement accuracy rather than plain facts
4. **Calibrates confidence**, which is the most important skill when there's negative marking

---

## 2. The 5 Question Levels

**L1**
- _Name_: Recall
- _Tests_: Facts: what, who, when, which Article/Act
- _Typical form_: One-line answer / simple MCQ
- _Share_: 25%

**L2**
- _Name_: Concept
- _Tests_: Why and how. Can you explain it in your own words?
- _Typical form_: "Explain…", "Why…", "What is the difference…"
- _Share_: 20%

**L3**
- _Name_: UPSC-Style
- _Tests_: Applying facts to exam format
- _Typical form_: Statement-based, "How many are correct", pair matching, statement-reason
- _Share_: 30%

**L4**
- _Name_: Analytical / Linkage
- _Tests_: Comparison, cause-effect, cross-subject, current-affairs link
- _Typical form_: "Evaluate…", "Link X to Y", case-based
- _Share_: 15%

**L5**
- _Name_: Trap / Myth-buster
- _Tests_: Common confusions, exceptions, extreme words
- _Typical form_: True/False with reason
- _Share_: 10%

---

## 3. The 10 Angles (each topic is covered from at least 5)

**A1.** **Definition / Core**
- _Sample prompt_: What exactly is X?

**A2.** **Origin / Source**
- _Sample prompt_: Where did it come from (Act, country, committee, person)?

**A3.** **Provisions / Features**
- _Sample prompt_: What does it contain?

**A4.** **Exceptions / Limits**
- _Sample prompt_: When does it NOT apply?

**A5.** **Comparison**
- _Sample prompt_: X vs Y: how do they differ?

**A6.** **Chronology**
- _Sample prompt_: What came first? Order these.

**A7.** **Authority / Body**
- _Sample prompt_: Who decides, enforces, or appoints?

**A8.** **Cause → Effect**
- _Sample prompt_: Why did it happen, and what did it lead to?

**A9.** **Current-Affairs Link**
- _Sample prompt_: Where has it been in the news recently?

**A10.** **Misconception**
- _Sample prompt_: What do most aspirants get wrong?

## 4. Structure of Every Weekly File

```
PART 0 – How to use this file (short)
PART A – Pre-Study Diagnostic (10 quick Qs, do BEFORE reading the week's topics)
PART B – Day-wise Question Sets (one block per topic, L1 → L5)
PART C – Sunday Mixed UPSC-Style Test (10–15 MCQs)
PART D – ANSWER KEY (only after attempting)
          • Answer
          • Explanation (why right AND why the other options are wrong)
          • 🧠 Concept behind it (the one-line takeaway)
          • ⚠️ Trap (where UPSC tries to fool you)
PART E – Self-Assessment Scorecard + Diagnosis
```

---

## 5. How to Attempt (Rules for You)

1. **Attempt before reading**: on the morning of each topic, give 15 min to that day's block cold.
2. **Mark confidence on every answer**:
   - **S** = Sure, **F** = 50-50 (Fifty), **G** = Guess
3. **Write a short answer, even for L2/L4** (2–4 bullet points). Saying it in your head doesn't count.
4. **Read the book/topic**, then re-attempt the questions you got wrong or marked F or G.
5. **Check the answer key only after the second attempt.**
6. **Log your score** in Part E and in the Progress Log below.

### Confidence calibration (the most important diagnostic)

**S but wrong**
- _Meaning_: **Dangerous.** False confidence loses marks to negative marking
- _Fix_: Add the item to the mistake register; re-read the source

**G but right**
- _Meaning_: Your knowledge is hazy, but your elimination works
- _Fix_: Revise the topic so it becomes S

**F mostly**
- _Meaning_: You're on the boundary
- _Fix_: One more revision usually converts these

**S and right**
- _Meaning_: Solid
- _Fix_: Spaced revision only

**Goal:** fewer than 10% of your S answers should be wrong. If more are wrong, you'll lose marks to over-attempting in the exam.

## 6. Diagnosis Bands (applies to each weekly file)

**≥ 80%**
- _Status_: 🟢 Strong
- _Action_: Move on, use spaced revision (Day 7, Day 21, Day 45)

**60–79%**
- _Status_: 🟡 Developing
- _Action_: Re-read only the weak sub-topics, re-attempt the questions you got wrong after 3 days

**40–59%**
- _Status_: 🟠 Shaky
- _Action_: Read the chapter again, make notes, do PYQs on that topic

**< 40%**
- _Status_: 🔴 Weak
- _Action_: Re-learn from the basics (NCERT/book), then do the whole set again next Sunday

**Look at levels separately too:**
- Low on L1 → not enough facts, so **read and revise more**
- Low on L2 → rote learning, so **explain each topic aloud in your own words**
- Low on L3 → weak at exam technique, so **do more PYQs and statement practice**
- Low on L4 → topics stay in silos, so **read current affairs and link it to static topics**
- Low on L5 → careless reading, so **underline words like only/all/never/always**

## 7. How Questions Are Built (for Claude: use this when creating each week)

For **each daily topic block**, create about 8–10 Polity/Economy-type questions or 6–8 History/Geography-type questions with this spread:
- 2–3 × L1 (different angles: A1, A2, A6, A7)
- 2 × L2 (A3, A5, A8)
- 2–3 × L3 (statement / how-many / pairs, mirroring UPSC 2023–2026 style)
- 1–2 × L4 (A5, A8, A9: link to current affairs and other subjects)
- 1 × L5 (A4, A10)

Rules:
- Every MCQ has 4 options. Include "How many of the above" questions (only one / only two / all three / none).
- Answers explain **why the wrong options are wrong**.
- Current-affairs links stay up to date (the last 12–18 months where possible).
- Questions go **in the notes first, answers at the end**, never mixed.
- Use verified facts only (standard sources: Laxmikanth, Spectrum, NCERT, Shankar, PIB).
- Keep a **Part A diagnostic** of 10 questions covering the whole week.
